Under the carpet

The thing no one wanted to talk about.

For decades, the conversation about girls, periods and toilets in Africa has been pushed under the carpet. Schools without working washrooms. Girls without underwear. Communities without water. A topic too uncomfortable for governments, donors and even families to face.

Sanitopia exists because silence is the cost. We're building the infrastructure — and the movement — to drag this issue into the open and fix it, school by school.

Read Richard's Story
  • Girls lose up to 20% of school days when their schools have no safe toilet.
  • Period stigma keeps families and schools from even discussing the problem.
  • One in three rural Ghanaian schools has no working washroom block at all.
  • Hygiene products remain out of reach for the girls who need them most.
  • Sanitopia is changing all of this — with toilets, underwear, water and noise.

Free Underwear · Women's hygiene

Period-safe underwear, delivered to schoolgirls in Ghana.

A women's-led mission

When a girl has underwear, she stays in class.

It sounds small. It changes everything. Across Ghana, thousands of schoolgirls miss days — sometimes weeks — of school every month, because the basics for managing a period with dignity simply aren't there.

Our Free Underwear programme is already on the ground in Ghana, distributing period-safe underwear directly to schoolgirls alongside the toilets we build. It's quiet work nobody else was doing — and we're making it loud.
