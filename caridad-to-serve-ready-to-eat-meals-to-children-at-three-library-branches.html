News / Caridad to Serve Ready-to-Eat Meals to Children at Three Library Branches

Caridad to serve ready to eat meals at three library branches.

A new partnership between Caridad Center and the Palm Beach County Library System brings free, nutritious meals directly to children at the places they already go to learn.

Public libraries are, quietly, one of the most reliable safety nets in American communities. They are warm, free, open to everyone, and staffed by people who already know the children of the neighbourhood by name. It makes a great deal of sense, then, that libraries are increasingly becoming places where children can also find a free, healthy meal.

Caridad Center, the largest free clinic in Florida and a long standing community health partner across Palm Beach County, has stepped into that role. In partnership with the Palm Beach County Library System, Caridad has begun distributing ready to eat meals to children at three library branches, expanding a model that has already proven its value across the county.

How the program works

Children who visit participating branches can pick up a free meal kit, no questions asked. The kits are designed to be ready to eat on the spot or to take home for later. There is no registration, no proof of income, and no stigma at the counter. A child who walks in for a book can walk out with both a story and a sandwich.

The program builds on the Palm Beach County Library System's broader social services work. Select branches across the county already hand out free meal kits to children on Fridays, working with local nonprofits, faith groups, and food banks to keep the supply moving.

Why Caridad is well suited to this

Caridad Center provides free medical, vision, dental, behavioural health, and social services to uninsured children and adults across Palm Beach County. Food access has always been part of how the organisation thinks about health. A child who eats well sleeps better, learns more, and recovers from illness faster. A clinic that takes nutrition seriously is a clinic that prevents disease rather than just treating it.

Bringing meals to library branches lets Caridad reach families who might not walk into a clinic. The library is neutral ground. It carries none of the perceived stigma some families attach to a soup kitchen or a charitable pantry.

Children deserve to eat in places that already feel safe to them. The library is one of those places.

Childhood food insecurity in South Florida

Even in one of the wealthiest counties in Florida, childhood food insecurity remains stubbornly present. National data continues to show elevated rates of hunger among children in lower income households, with summer breaks and weekends representing the periods of greatest risk because school meal programs do not run.

Partnership programs like Caridad's library distribution effort exist precisely to plug those gaps. A meal handed to a child at a library on a Friday afternoon is a meal that does not have to be improvised over the weekend.

A model worth replicating

The Caridad partnership is a useful case study for community organisations elsewhere. The ingredients are simple. A trusted nonprofit with food access experience. A public institution with established foot traffic among the population in need. A reliable supply of safe, ready to eat meals. And a shared commitment to making the program feel like ordinary hospitality rather than charity.

If it works in Palm Beach County, there is no reason it cannot work in dozens of other counties facing the same gap between school meals and home meals. Libraries are everywhere. Hunger, unfortunately, is everywhere too. The match is obvious once someone takes the trouble to make it.
