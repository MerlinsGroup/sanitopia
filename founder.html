The beginning

From Nkonya to the UK — and back.

Richard Kojo Acheampong attended Nkonya Senior High School in Ghana's Oti Region. So did his wife, Joan. The school gave them their foundation — but when they returned years later, they found the facilities in crisis.

Richard had moved to the UK in 1998. A severe car accident resulted in the amputation of his foot. While still in hospital, he faced deportation threats. Unable to find conventional employment, he started his first business in 2002. Today, his ventures employ hundreds in the UK economy.

In 2016, he qualified as a dual lawyer — Supreme Court of Ghana and Senior Courts of England and Wales. Success in the UK, but his heart remained in Ghana.

The moment everything changed

Visiting Nkonya SHS with Joan, they saw the abandoned washrooms. The girls' facility hadn't worked for over a decade. A 25-unit block stood half-finished. Students were suffering in silence.

Richard and Joan decided to act. Using personal savings and proceeds from Richard's book "Crushed But Not Destroyed", they funded the reconstruction. What started as a $15,000 project grew to over $30,000 as they added a roofed walkway and proper water systems.

120 days later, 37 toilet units were complete and commissioned. The pilot proved the model worked.

Why Sanitopia exists

That first project at Nkonya wasn't enough. Richard saw the same problem at schools across Ghana. Libraries without toilets. Health centres with broken facilities. Communities suffering needlessly.

Sanitopia was born from a simple belief: communities that give us everything deserve something back. Richard personally invested £20,000 to launch the organisation, on top of the £24,000 already spent at Nkonya.

Now, with a country-wide partnership signed with the Ghana Library Authority to build 150 facilities for public libraries, Sanitopia is scaling Richard's vision: 20,000 modern sanitation facilities across Africa, starting with at least 1,000 across Ghana by 2030.
