Founder · Sanitopia
Richard Kojo Acheampong

From a Ghanaian village to the courts of England — and back again.

Originally from Ghana, Richard relocated to the UK in 1998. Shortly after he arrived, a severe car accident resulted in the amputation of one of his feet. The trauma tested his physical resilience — and the difficult immigration aftermath, which included repeated threats of deportation while still hospitalised, tested everything else.

Unable to find conventional employment, he started his first business in 2002. Two decades later, his ventures have created jobs for hundreds of people in the UK economy.

He went back to school. In 2016 he qualified as a lawyer — dual-qualified as a Lawyer of the Supreme Court of Ghana and a Solicitor of the Senior Courts of England and Wales. He turned his story into a memoir, Crushed But Not Destroyed, and used the proceeds to fund Sanitopia's first project.

We all must give back to our communities. That belief is the foundation of everything Sanitopia builds.

A personal commitment

Richard and his wife, Joan Asare-Acheampong, are both alumni of Nkonya Senior High School. Their pilot project — a 12-seater girls' washroom plus the completion of a long-abandoned 25-unit facility — was funded out of personal savings and book royalties. Originally budgeted at GHS 210,000 (~$15,000), the final cost reached GHS 420,000 (over $30,000) once additional works were folded in. They delivered it in 120 days.

Richard's personal donation of £20,000 ($24,863) launched Sanitopia, on top of the £24,132 ($30,000) already invested in the pilot. That early funding secured legal counsel, expert advisors, vital resources and a dedicated team.
