Alumni couple funds modern sanitation at Nkonya SHS.

Richard and Joan Acheampong personally invested around thirty thousand dollars to rebuild 37 units of sanitation at their old school, delivered in 120 days as promised.

When Richard Kojo Acheampong returned to walk the grounds of Nkonya Senior High School in the Oti Region of Ghana, decades after leaving as a student, he expected to feel nostalgia. Instead he felt alarm. The washrooms used by hundreds of girls were makeshift, unsafe, and a daily indignity for students who deserved better. He decided that day to do something about it.

Richard is a dual qualified lawyer of the Supreme Court of Ghana and the Senior Courts of England and Wales. His wife, Joan Asare Acheampong, attended Nkonya SHS alongside him and served as Girls' Prefect during her time there. They are, in the simplest terms, the school's own children returning home.

A 37 unit rebuild in 120 days

The couple committed to building a modern 12 seater toilet facility specifically for the girls' dormitory, refurbishing an existing washroom block, and adding a roofed walkway connecting the new facility to an existing 25 unit bathroom so that students could move safely between blocks even in heavy rain. Together that adds up to a 37 unit modernisation of the school's sanitation footprint.

They set a hard deadline of 120 days from groundbreaking to handover, and they met it. The total personal investment came to around thirty thousand US dollars, paid out of the couple's own pocket and supplemented by Richard's writing royalties.

Why they did it themselves

Richard has spoken publicly about the choice to fund the project personally rather than waiting for institutional support. Government budgets are tight. Donor cycles are slow. Girls were missing school every month, and many were being put at risk by the existing facilities. The simplest moral arithmetic told him that delay would cost more than action.

If you can walk past a problem at the school that raised you, you should not be surprised when nothing changes. We chose not to walk past.

The seed of Sanitopia

What started as a personal project for one school became the founding act of Sanitopia. The Nkonya intervention demonstrated that a carefully scoped, professionally engineered, and tightly managed sanitation project could be delivered on time and on budget by a small team that cared about the outcome.

Out of that conviction came Sanitopia, a UK Community Interest Company and a registered non-profit in Ghana, with a stated mission to deliver 10,000 sanitation projects across Africa by 2035. The Nkonya project is now project number one, and the model for everything that follows.

Reception at the school

School leadership and the wider Nkonya community have publicly thanked the couple for their commitment. Teachers report improved attendance among female students. Parents report higher confidence in the school environment. And girls themselves report something simpler and more important. They have a clean, safe, private place to go.

The Acheampongs see the Nkonya project as a personal debt repaid and a public template proven. The work, for them, is just beginning.
